# 07 - Six Sigma DMAIC (simulated exercise)

> **SIMULATED DATA / HYPOTHETICAL BUSINESS.** This is an independent portfolio case study about a fictional retailer ("NorthBay Mart"). It uses no Amazon data, metrics or processes, and makes no claim about any real operation. Scenario results are *modelled estimates*, not measured improvements.

> This is a **simulated DMAIC exercise** on synthetic data, an application of the framework to a hypothetical operation. It is **not** a real company process-improvement engagement and makes no claim about delivered results.

## Define

**Problem statement.** During the 7-day "Winter Mega Sale", the hypothetical retailer's customer-support plan fails to hold service levels: event-week SLA is 67.4% against an 80% target, and 3 of 7 event days meet it.

**Business objective.** Hold at least 80% of contacts answered within 300 seconds on at least 6 of 7 event days, with no end-of-day backlog above 100 contacts, within existing approval limits.

**Critical-to-quality (CTQ).** (1) Speed of answer (SLA); (2) no standing backlog; (3) staffing matched to workload by queue and hour; (4) forecast reliable enough to staff against.

**Scope.** Customer-contact staffing and forecasting for two queues (OD, RP) over 14 days. Out of scope: fulfilment redesign, pricing/promotions, tooling.

**SIPOC (high level).**

| Supplier | Input | Process | Output | Customer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fulfilment Ops, Inventory, Marketing, Finance | Orders, exceptions, stock-outs, promo calendar, approvals | Forecast contacts -> compute requirement -> roster -> operate -> monitor/re-forecast | Answered contacts within SLA | Shoppers; Leadership |

## Measure

Defect definition: a contact not answered within 300s (arrival-weighted from the hourly queue model).

| Measure | Baseline week (B1-B7) | Event week (E1-E7) |
|---|---|---|
| Contacts handled | 11,275 | 26,461 |
| Defects (contacts not answered within threshold) | 843 | 8,634 |
| Defect rate | 7.5% | 32.6% |
| DPMO | 74,798 | 326,308 |
| Sigma level (1.5-shift convention, illustrative) | 2.94 | 1.95 |
| SLA attainment | 92.5% | 67.4% |

The sigma level uses the conventional 1.5-sigma shift and is illustrative only. Baseline-week performance shows the plan works at normal volume; the event week is where it fails.

Other baseline measures (all from the simulated data): contact forecast WAPE 11.5% / bias -7.0% (event week); 56 of 224 event group-hours understaffed; worst day E2 at 37.6%; E7 close backlog 400 contacts.

## Analyze

Plausible root causes, each tied to modelled evidence (and what the evidence does *not* show):

| # | Root cause | Evidence in the simulation | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Contact-mix forecast error.** The forecast applies baseline contacts-per-order to forecast orders | OD workload +24% vs forecast; RP +1%; category biases range from returns +51% to delivery delay -34% | Planted by design; real mix error would need to be measured |
| 2 | **Lagged contact drivers not in the plan.** Exceptions and stock-outs generate contacts 1-2 days later | E1 SLA held but E2 collapsed; exceptions peaked at 6,911 on E7; delay contacts +229% vs baseline day | Lags are model assumptions |
| 3 | **Siloed queues.** Surplus in RP cannot serve OD | RP surplus 544 agent-hours while OD short 246 gross | Cross-skill feasibility not tested |
| 4 | **Weekly balance masks hourly/daily gaps** | OD net gap about zero for the week yet 46 understaffed hours | Hourly rounding inflates small-volume gaps |
| 5 | **Roster shape.** Few shift templates; one template covers the 19:00 peak | Top short hours: 19:00, 20:00, 11:00; reallocating within templates (S1) changed SLA -0.5 pp | Template set is a design choice |
| 6 | **Handling-time drift (incl. mix shift) and absence** | Workload variance: volume +153 vs AHT +142 agent-hours; event absence 6.4% of shifts vs 6% planned (E3 spike 12%) | The queue-level "AHT effect" combines the +5% complexity factor with a shift toward longer-handling categories (e.g. delivery delay); the two are not separated. Absence alone is a minor contributor here |

Of the event-week workload variance, volume explains 52% and queue-level AHT (complexity plus category-mix shift) 48%. SLA is generated by a queue model from staffing and load, so the staffing-to-service link is true by construction; the *relative size* of the causes is a property of the assumptions.

## Improve

Seven options compared across 200 Monte Carlo replications. **All results are modelled estimates**; no cost, productivity or SLA improvement is claimed for any real operation.

| ID | Option | Mean event SLA % | p10 | p90 | Change vs S0 (pp) | End backlog | Extra paid agent-h |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S0 | As-planned baseline | 63.5 | 50.5 | 75.4 | +0.0 | 174 | 0 |
| S1 | Reallocate existing staff to rolling-corrected forecast | 63.0 | 49.5 | 75.3 | -0.5 | 163 | 0 |
| S2 | Cross-train 20% of agents across both queues | 72.2 | 61.2 | 82.9 | +8.7 | 113 | 0 |
| S3 | Extra peak-cover capacity (17:00-22:00), spread evenly over queues | 77.4 | 65.8 | 88.1 | +14.0 | 48 | 420 |
| S4 | Same extra capacity, targeted at the bottleneck queue (OD) | 79.8 | 69.4 | 88.0 | +16.3 | 15 | 420 |
| S5 | Combined: S1 + S2 + S4 | 84.9 | 77.5 | 91.4 | +21.4 | 4 | 420 |
| S6 | Ablation: S2 + S4 (no reallocation) | 85.7 | 78.0 | 91.9 | +22.2 | 4 | 420 |

Selection logic (a simple impact vs cost/feasibility view, not a financial model):

- **Pilot first:** S2 cross-training: +8.7 pp, no extra paid hours, but unmodelled training cost and lead time.
- **Approve if budget allows:** S4 targeted capacity: +16.3 pp for +420 paid agent-hours; better than the same hours spread evenly (S3 +14.0 pp).
- **Best modelled outcome:** S6 (S2 + S4): mean 85.7% SLA, 5.1 of 7 days meeting target.
- **Do not rely on:** S1 reallocation within the current shift templates (no measurable gain).
- Even the best option does not reach target in every replication (p10 78%), so the control plan below still matters.

## Control

Monitoring thresholds, owners, cadence and escalation (hypothetical roles):

| Metric | Threshold | Owner (role) | Cadence | Escalation rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Daily SLA attainment (by queue) | >= 80%; amber < 80%; red < 60% | Customer Support Ops Manager | Hourly during event; daily review | Two consecutive hours < 70% in a queue -> Program Manager; red day -> Sponsor same day |
| End-of-day backlog | <= 100 contacts amber at 100, red at 150 | Customer Support Ops Manager | Daily 22:30 | Red -> activate recovery roster and overflow capacity |
| Queue-hour capacity gap | 0 agents short; amber 1-2; red >= 3 | Workforce Planning Lead | Twice daily (10:00, 16:00) | Same hour short on 3 consecutive days -> roster change request |
| Contact forecast error (day-to-date, by queue) | within +/-10% | Workforce Planning Analyst | Twice daily | Outside +/-10% -> apply rolling correction; > +/-20% -> escalate |
| Fulfilment exceptions (daily count) | <= 2x baseline-day average | Fulfilment Ops Manager | Daily 10:00 feed | > 2x -> lagged contact uplift added to next-day forecast |
| Absence rate | <= 8% of scheduled shifts | Customer Support Ops Manager | Daily 08:00 | > 10% -> standby pool / shift swaps |
| AHT vs plan | within +5% | Customer Support Quality Lead | Daily | > 8% -> macro/FAQ fix; update planning AHT |
| Cross-trained headcount | >= 20% certified by T-7 | Customer Support Training Manager | Weekly until T-7 | < 15% at T-7 -> Sponsor |

**Review cadence.** Hourly queue check by the Ops Manager during the event; twice-daily forecast review; daily 22:30 status to the Program Manager; weekly executive readout; post-event review at T+7 with the control plan handed to a named owner.

**Sustaining the gain (if this were real).** Update planning AHT and contact rates by queue from the event; keep the lag-aware forecast; re-run scenarios before the next peak; verify that thresholds would have triggered: in this run OD SLA on E2 was 7% (below the 70% trigger) and OD backlog at close was 126 contacts (above the 100-contact amber line).
